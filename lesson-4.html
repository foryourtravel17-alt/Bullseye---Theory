Market Structure & Price Action

Candlesticks, bullish and bearish markets, trends, ranges, support and resistance.

Study Sections

1. Candlestick basics

Each candle summarizes open, high, low and close during a chosen timeframe. The body shows the open-to-close relationship; the wicks show the extremes reached during that interval. A bullish candle only means the close was above the open for that interval—it does not prove the larger market is in an uptrend. Read candles alongside nearby swings, levels and timeframe.

2. Bullish vs bearish

Bullish and bearish describe direction or expectations, but should not be treated as certainty. A bullish candle may form inside a broader downtrend, and a bearish candle may appear during a larger uptrend. Distinguish the candle’s direction, broader structure and your hypothesis. Good chart notes state what price did rather than claiming what it must do next.

3. Uptrends and downtrends

An uptrend commonly forms higher swing highs and higher swing lows; a downtrend commonly forms lower swing highs and lower swing lows. These are structural descriptions, not guarantees. Decide what counts as a meaningful swing before marking it, and use the same rule across the chart. A single pullback does not automatically end a trend.

4. Sideways/range markets

A range forms when price repeatedly moves between approximate upper and lower boundaries without sustained directional progress. The middle can offer unclear risk/reward because price can move either way. Boundaries can break, and apparent ranges can differ across timeframes. Mark boundaries as zones and consider rejection, consolidation or breakout instead of assuming the range will hold.

5. Support and resistance

Support is an area where price previously attracted buying or slowed a decline; resistance is an area where selling or hesitation appeared. These are zones of interest, not walls that guarantee reversals. Reactions depend on context, volatility, liquidity and how price approaches the area. State what would invalidate your interpretation before using a zone in a hypothetical plan.

Lesson Summary & Key Takeaways

Price action describes what price has done, while market structure organizes that movement into swings and ranges. Candles summarize open, high, low and close for a selected interval; candle color alone does not establish a trend. An uptrend commonly shows successive higher highs and higher lows, while a downtrend commonly shows lower highs and lower lows. In a range, price rotates between areas rather than making sustained progress. Support and resistance are best treated as zones where reactions have occurred, not exact lines that must hold. A level can fail, and context plus a defined invalidation point matters more than a single candle.

Apply it: For each chart example, separate observation from interpretation, name the uncertainty, and state what evidence would invalidate your view. This is educational material, not personalized financial advice.
