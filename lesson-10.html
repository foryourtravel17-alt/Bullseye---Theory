Trading Psychology & Discipline

Emotions, routine, revenge trading, journaling, daily structure and wellbeing.

Study Sections

1. Emotions

Emotions such as fear, greed, frustration and overconfidence can affect entries, exits and position size. The goal is not to eliminate feelings but to notice when they are driving a decision instead of the written plan. A pause or checklist can help separate evidence from impulse. Record emotional state alongside the decision so patterns become visible over time.

2. Routine

A routine creates structure before, during and after a session. Preparation can include checking the asset, timeframe, major events and personal readiness; execution uses predefined criteria and risk limits; review records what happened and what can be learned. A routine should also include breaks and a clear stop time. More screen time does not automatically create better decisions.

3. Revenge trading

Revenge trading means taking a new risk mainly to recover a previous loss, often without the original setup or normal risk checks. This can create a cycle of emotional decisions and escalating exposure. After a loss, step away and review whether the plan was followed before considering another hypothetical setup. The market does not owe a recovery, and a missed trade is preferable to breaking a risk rule.

4. Journal

A useful journal records date, asset, timeframe, setup, evidence, planned entry/exit, risk, actual execution, costs and emotional state. Include screenshots or notes before and after the decision to reduce hindsight bias. Review a series of trades rather than judging a method from one outcome. Separate process quality from financial result: a disciplined trade can lose, and an undisciplined trade can win by chance.

5. Daily structure

Daily structure should separate preparation, execution and review so that each has a clear purpose. Set boundaries for screen time, risk, breaks and when to stop; fatigue and stress can reduce judgment. If you feel unable to follow your own limits, pause rather than forcing a trade. Trading education should support financial wellbeing, not interfere with sleep, relationships or essential responsibilities.

Lesson Summary & Key Takeaways

Psychology affects whether a trader follows a plan consistently. Fear, greed, overconfidence and frustration can lead to impulsive entries, premature exits or increasing risk after losses. Revenge trading attempts to recover a loss quickly and often abandons the original process. A written routine can include preparation, a checklist, predefined risk limits, breaks and a post-session review. A journal should record the setup, context, entry/exit reasoning, risk, execution quality and emotions—not only profit or loss. Evaluate decisions across a meaningful sample; one winning trade does not prove a good process, and one losing trade does not automatically prove a bad one.
