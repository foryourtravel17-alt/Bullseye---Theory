Breakouts & Retests

Breakouts, retests, possible outcomes, false breakouts and evidence checklist.

Study Sections

1. Breakout

A breakout occurs when price moves beyond a previously watched range or zone. A brief wick outside the boundary is not the same as sustained acceptance beyond it; the close, follow-through and surrounding context may add evidence. Breakouts can fail quickly, especially when liquidity is thin or many participants react to the same level. Describe what happened first before assigning a bullish or bearish meaning.

2. Retest

A retest is a later return toward the area price has broken through. The former resistance may act as support, or the area may fail and price may return to the range; a retest may not happen at all. Do not assume a touch alone confirms the setup. Define the area, the evidence needed and the point that would invalidate the idea before evaluating the example.

3. Possible outcomes

After a breakout, price can continue in the breakout direction, consolidate, retest the boundary, or fall back into the prior range. These are scenarios, not guaranteed stages of a pattern. A trader who plans for only one outcome may ignore evidence that contradicts the original idea. Record the alternative outcomes and the maximum planned risk before drawing a conclusion.

4. False breakout

A false breakout occurs when price moves outside a boundary but then returns inside, suggesting the move did not sustain. It can trap participants who assume that crossing a level guarantees continuation. No single clue identifies every false breakout in advance; liquidity, volatility, timeframe and follow-through all matter. Study failures as carefully as successful breakouts and avoid hindsight-based certainty.

5. Evidence checklist

An evidence checklist can include the exact boundary, candle close, follow-through, broader structure, liquidity conditions, transaction costs and a clear invalidation point. No checklist makes a breakout certain, but it can reduce impulsive decisions and improve consistency. Compare examples across different conditions and record whether the rules were followed. Do not treat this lesson as a live trading signal.

Lesson Summary & Key Takeaways

A breakout occurs when price moves beyond a watched boundary; a retest is a later return toward that area. After a breakout, price may continue, retest and continue, consolidate, or fall back into the prior range. False breakouts happen, particularly when liquidity is thin or participants quickly reject prices outside the boundary. A close beyond a level, follow-through, market context and a clear invalidation point can add evidence, but cannot guarantee continuation. Avoid treating every touch as a signal. Study examples that succeeded and failed, including transaction costs and the possibility of entering after most of the move has already occurred.

Apply it: For each chart example, separate observation from interpretation, name the uncertainty, and state what evidence would invalidate your view. This is educational material, not personalized financial advice.
