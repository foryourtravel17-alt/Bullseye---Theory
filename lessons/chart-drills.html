DRILL 01 / LESSON 01

Trading basics and P&L

No transaction costs included

You buy 10 units at 100 and later sell all 10 at 104. What is the gross result before fees, spread and slippage? Name one reason the realised result could differ.

Write the calculation and one cost/risk factor.
Review answer

Gross P&L = (104 − 100) × 10 = +40 currency units before costs. Fees, spread, slippage, partial fills or a different exit price can change the realised result. A positive example is not a promise of profit.

DRILL 02 / LESSON 02

Buyers, sellers and price discovery

The best bid is 99.9 and the best ask is 100.1. What does the spread represent, and which side is willing to buy versus sell?

Explain the two prices in one sentence.
Review answer

The bid is the highest displayed price buyers are willing to pay; the ask is the lowest displayed price sellers are willing to accept. The spread is ask minus bid = 0.2. Actual execution can differ as orders change.

DRILL 03 / LESSON 03

Read a forex pair

EUR/USD moves from 1.0800 to 1.0900. Which currency is the base currency, which is the quote currency, and what does the rise mean for the euro relative to the dollar?

Do not describe this as a guaranteed future move.
Review answer

EUR is the base currency and USD is the quote currency. The quoted number is dollars per euro. A rise means one euro buys more dollars at that quoted rate. It does not prove what will happen next.

DRILL 04 / LESSON 04

Read candle anatomy

For the candle shown, identify the open, high, low and close. Is the candle bullish or bearish? What does the upper wick show?

State the body direction and both extremes.
Review answer

Open 100, high 108, low 97, close 105. It is bullish because close is above open. The upper wick spans 105 to 108 and records the interval's high above the close; it does not by itself predict reversal.

DRILL 05 / LESSON 05

Pattern needs context

The second candle's real body fully covers the first candle's real body. What pattern might this describe, and what additional context should be checked before interpreting it?

Name two context checks.
Review answer

It may describe a bullish engulfing pattern. Check where it occurs relative to structure or a meaningful zone, the completed candle close, volume/liquidity, nearby resistance and a clear invalidation point. The pattern is descriptive, not a standalone entry signal.

DRILL 06 / LESSON 06

Mark trend structure

Label the swing sequence. What combination of swing highs and swing lows is consistent with an uptrend? What would weaken that reading?

Write the structure shorthand.
Review answer

An uptrend is commonly described by higher highs (HH) and higher lows (HL). A failure to hold a prior higher low or a sequence of lower highs/lower lows can weaken or change the structure reading. Wait for confirmed swings; do not relabel hindsight as certainty.

DRILL 07 / LESSON 07

Mark a support/resistance zone

Price reacts near the same area several times. Why is it better to treat the area as a zone rather than an exact price? State one condition that would invalidate the idea.

Define the zone and its invalidation.
Review answer

Orders and reactions occur across a range, not a perfectly exact price. A clear close and follow-through beyond the zone, defined in advance, can invalidate the zone hypothesis. A touch alone is not a guaranteed reversal.

DRILL 08 / LESSON 08

Breakout, retest or false break?

A candle's wick moves above resistance but closes back inside the range. Is that a confirmed close-based breakout? What evidence would you wait for?

Distinguish a wick from a completed close.
Review answer

No. A wick above the boundary that closes back inside is not a close-based breakout. Wait for a completed close beyond the zone and assess follow-through, volume, retest behavior, costs and invalidation. A later return inside can indicate a false break.

DRILL 09 / LESSON 09

Calculate risk and position size

A hypothetical account is ₹50,000 and the chosen risk budget is 1%. Entry is 250 and stop is 245, so risk per share is ₹5. Calculate the risk budget, maximum quantity before costs and a 2R target price.

Show all three calculations.
Review answer

Risk budget = ₹50,000 × 1% = ₹500. Quantity = ₹500 ÷ ₹5 = 100 shares before costs and slippage. A 2R target is entry + 2 × ₹5 = ₹260. This is a maths exercise, not a recommended trade or a guarantee that the stop will fill at its displayed price.

DRILL 10 / LESSON 10

Respond to a loss without revenge trading

After two planned losses, you feel pressure to increase size to recover. What should your process do next? Name two journal fields to review.

Write a rule that prevents revenge trading.
Review answer

Pause and follow the pre-written daily loss or trade-count limit; do not increase risk to recover losses. Review setup quality, rule adherence, entry/exit, risk, market context, emotions and execution costs.

DRILL 11 / LESSON 11

Combine the complete framework

Before any hypothetical trade idea, write the higher-timeframe context, the zone, the completed-candle trigger, the entry, stop, target and the condition that invalidates the idea. What should happen if one required part is missing?

Create a five-step checklist, then state the no-trade rule.
Review answer

1) Read structure and market context. 2) Mark a meaningful zone. 3) Wait for the defined completed-candle trigger. 4) Calculate entry, stop, target and risk before acting. 5) Journal and review the outcome and process. If a required condition is missing, do not force a trade; wait for a complete setup.
