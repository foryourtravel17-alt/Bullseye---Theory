LESSON 3 / 10

Crypto & Forex Foundations

Cryptocurrency, forex, currency values and a basic forex example.

Study Sections

1. Cryptocurrency

Cryptocurrency is a digital asset that may use a blockchain or another distributed ledger to record transfers. Different tokens have different purposes and risk profiles; price can be affected by liquidity, concentrated ownership, exchange access, security incidents and regulation. Identify the asset and venue, check liquidity and understand custody/platform risks before studying its chart. A rising chart does not prove an asset is fundamentally sound.

2. Forex

Forex is the market for exchanging one currency for another. Quotes are shown as pairs: the base currency is first and the quote currency is second, while bid/ask prices create a spread. Currency prices can respond to interest-rate expectations, inflation, central-bank decisions, economic releases and geopolitical risk. Leverage can magnify both gains and losses, so learn contract size and margin rules before interpreting an example.

3. Currency values

A currency pair shows relative value, not the absolute value of one currency in isolation. If EUR/USD rises, one euro buys more dollars than before, all else equal; that does not mean every euro-related pair must rise. Both currencies respond to changing expectations, so avoid explaining every move with a single headline. Always specify the pair, direction and timeframe when describing a price change.

4. Hypothetical forex example

Treat any currency example as a calculation exercise, not a prediction. Identify whether the position is long or short, calculate the quote movement, and translate it using position size and instrument specifications. Spread, commissions, financing, slippage and currency conversion can change the net result. A favorable price move can still lose money if direction or costs are misunderstood.

5. Review

You should be able to explain the difference between a cryptocurrency asset and a forex pair, and read a base/quote quote correctly. Practice by writing what a rising pair means, what could move it, and which costs or risks are missing from a simplified example. Check your explanation against bullish and bearish scenarios. No single market label makes an asset safe or a trade certain.

Lesson Summary & Key Takeaways

Forex trades currency pairs: the first currency is the base and the second is the quote. A quote such as EUR/USD expresses how many units of the quote currency one unit of the base currency buys; a rising quote means the base currency has strengthened relative to the quote currency, all else equal. Crypto is a broad category, not one uniform asset class: tokens can differ sharply in purpose, liquidity, custody, governance and risk. Both markets can move rapidly and trade on different venues with different protections. Understand spread, volatility, liquidity, counterparty/platform risk and position exposure; examples in this lesson are hypothetical, not recommendations.

Apply it: For each chart example, separate observation from interpretation, name the uncertainty, and state what evidence would invalidate your view. This is educational material, not personalized financial advice.

Five concept-based questions. Pass mark: 4/5. Read each scenario carefully; correct answers are mixed across options.

2. What is the spread in a forex quote?

3. Why can crypto assets carry different risks?

4. If EUR/USD rises, what generally happened?
