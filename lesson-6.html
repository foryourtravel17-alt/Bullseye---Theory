LESSON 6 / 11

Trend Structure & Market Reading

HH, HL, LH, LL, ranges, chart reading and structure shifts.

Study Sections

1. Higher highs and higher lows

Higher highs and higher lows are a common sequence in an uptrend: each meaningful swing high exceeds the prior one and pullbacks hold above prior swing lows. This structure suggests buyers have been able to push price farther upward, but it does not guarantee continuation. Define swings consistently and avoid labeling every tiny fluctuation as a structural point. A later break of a meaningful higher low may warn that conditions are changing.

2. Lower highs and lower lows

Lower highs and lower lows are a common sequence in a downtrend. Sellers have pushed price to progressively lower lows while rebounds fail below prior swing highs. The sequence can pause or reverse, so treat it as a description of past behavior rather than a forecast. Record which swing would need to break before your bearish interpretation changes.

3. Range structure

A range occurs when price repeatedly reacts within upper and lower boundaries without a clear sequence of higher highs/higher lows or lower highs/lower lows. Ranges can contain smaller trends and false breaks, so mark their edges as zones rather than exact prices. The middle often gives less clarity about direction. Keep both breakout and rejection outcomes in mind.

4. Chart reading

Read a chart in a repeatable order: identify the asset and timeframe, note the broad condition, mark meaningful swing points, then describe where current price sits relative to them. Separate observable facts from interpretations. Avoid adding indicators or labels simply to make a chart look certain. A useful chart note explains the evidence, the uncertainty and what would change the conclusion.

5. Structure shifts

A structure shift is a possible change in the sequence of swing highs and lows. A wick beyond a swing level can be temporary; a close beyond it and follow-through may provide additional evidence, though no confirmation is infallible. Use the same swing rules before and after the move to avoid hindsight bias. State what would invalidate the new interpretation and consider the larger timeframe.

Lesson Summary & Key Takeaways

Structure reading follows the sequence of meaningful swing highs and lows. Higher highs and higher lows are evidence consistent with an uptrend; lower highs and lower lows are evidence consistent with a downtrend. Repeated reactions within boundaries can indicate a range. A break of a prior swing may signal a change in behavior, but a wick through a level is not automatically a confirmed shift; candle closes, follow-through and the timeframe being studied all matter. Mark swings consistently, avoid relabeling them after the fact, and separate an observation (“the prior low broke”) from an interpretation (“momentum may be changing”).

Apply it: For each chart example, separate observation from interpretation, name the uncertainty, and state what evidence would invalidate your view. This is educational material, not personalized financial advice.
