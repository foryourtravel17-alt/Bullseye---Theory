Candlestick Patterns

Candle anatomy and the Doji, Hammer, Shooting Star, Engulfing and Star patterns.

Study Sections

1. Candle anatomy

The candle body spans the open and close, while the upper and lower wicks show the period’s high and low beyond the body. A large body may indicate a strong move during that interval, but does not reveal every participant’s intent. A long wick can show rejection or volatility, yet its meaning depends on where it appears. Compare it with surrounding price action.

2. Doji

A doji has an open and close that are very close relative to the candle’s total range. It can reflect temporary balance or indecision during that interval, but does not automatically signal a reversal. A doji after a strong move near a meaningful zone may deserve attention; the same shape in a noisy range may have little value. Observe later price behavior before deciding what it means.

3. Hammer and Shooting Star

A hammer-like candle usually has a small body toward the top of its range and a longer lower wick; a shooting-star-like candle has a small body toward the bottom and a longer upper wick. These shapes describe price travel and close, not a guaranteed future direction. Location and prior movement matter. Define confirmation and invalidation rather than trading the name alone.

4. Engulfing

An engulfing pattern compares the real bodies of consecutive candles, where the later body covers the earlier body under the chosen definition. It can show a change in short-term pressure, but does not guarantee a broader trend reversal. Note surrounding structure, candle size, volatility and proximity to a zone. Pattern definitions vary, so use one consistent rule.

5. Morning and Evening Star

Morning-star and evening-star patterns are multi-candle formations often studied for possible shifts after a directional move. Interpretation depends on sequence, location and whether later price action supports the idea. Exact candle rules can differ across markets and timeframes, so define the version being studied. Review both successful and failed examples to avoid hindsight bias.

Lesson Summary & Key Takeaways

A candlestick’s body shows the distance between open and close; its wicks show the period’s extremes. Doji candles suggest a close near the open, while hammer-like and shooting-star-like shapes describe where the body and wick sit within the candle’s range. Engulfing and morning/evening-star formations describe multi-candle relationships. These patterns are descriptive, not predictive guarantees: their meaning depends on location, preceding movement, timeframe, volatility and confirmation. A pattern in the middle of noise may have little value. Practice labeling the candle objectively, then explain the context and what evidence would prove the interpretation wrong.
